# Jason's texting style

Jason explicitly requested this preference on September 13, 2026, based on his outgoing Google Voice messages. Use this style by default for his text drafts. These are observed preferences, not rigid templates; adapt to the recipient and Jason's latest instructions.

## Shared voice

- Brief, warm, conversational, and direct. Usually one to three short sentences, with the practical point early.
- For appointment requests: a short greeting, identify Jason if needed, then ask about the day and time/window. Existing threads often need only the request.
- Use everyday words and natural contractions. Avoid formal letter openings, corporate phrasing, excessive explanation, and routine long signatures.
- Familiar replies can be very short: "Sounds good!", "Okay, confirmed", "On my way!", "Ty!", or "No worries". An occasional smiley or laugh fits a familiar conversation; do not add one to every message.
- When late: brief apology and revised arrival time. When rescheduling: ask directly and courteously. For concerns: a short empathetic response plus the next practical step, only when actually known.
- Preserve clear dates, times, recipients, and meaning. Do not deliberately reproduce accidental typos, wrong numbers, or ambiguous scheduling details to mimic informality.

## English

Use "Hi [name], it's Jason PT" or "Hi, this is Jason Ngo, physical therapist" when an introduction helps. Familiar recipients get "Hi [name]!" or just the message.

Natural phrasing includes "Can I come ...?", "Is [patient] available ...?", "around", "closer to", "I'll aim for", and "See you then". Time windows are common, but only use windows or times Jason supplied or that are verified. A calendar slot and an arrival window are different; do not invent one from the other.

## Vietnamese

Use natural, respectful conversational Vietnamese. Jason commonly mixes English terms such as "PT", "physical therapy", and "slot" into Vietnamese and sometimes writes without accents. Default to readable accented Vietnamese while matching the existing thread when useful; do not turn it into a formal translation.

Check Jason's previous outgoing messages to the specific recipient before choosing Vietnamese forms of address. Preserve the established pair, such as "chị/em" or "cô/con", and how he refers to the patient, such as "bà". Do not default all older family contacts to "cô/con" or guess from names or family roles. Jason uses "ghé", "tầm", "khoảng", "được không", "nha chị", and "cảm ơn"; add "ạ" when it matches that relationship. In a familiar exchange, a short confirmation is enough.

## Illustrative drafts

These examples show tone, not real appointments or promises:

- English request: "Hi [name], it's Jason PT. Can I come tomorrow around [time]?"
- English delay: "Hi [name], I'm running behind. I'll be there closer to [time]. Sorry!"
- Vietnamese request: "Chào cô, con là Jason PT. Mai con ghé tầm [giờ] được không ạ?"
- Vietnamese delay: "Xin lỗi cô, con tới trễ. Con sẽ ghé khoảng [giờ]."

## Appointment texting flow

1. Find the existing conversation and verify the recipient's number against the patient's contact information. Read Jason's prior outgoing texts to match language, forms of address, and familiarity before drafting.
2. Use the day and time options Jason requested. If he offers two alternatives, phrase them as alternatives, not a continuous arrival window. Make noon versus afternoon clear in Vietnamese.
3. When Jason asks to review first, show each draft labeled with the patient and actual recipient, and keep it unsent. Apply any corrections to the draft.
4. When Jason approves the drafts and says to send (including "yes to both now text"), send the latest approved version to each verified recipient without asking for the same approval again. Do not silently rewrite approved wording.
5. Verify each outgoing message appears in the correct thread and no longer shows "Sending..." or an error. A click alone is not proof it was sent. If status is uncertain, inspect the thread before retrying to avoid duplicate texts.
6. Report briefly which messages were sent. Sending an appointment request does not mean the patient confirmed a time; do not mark the calendar confirmed solely because the text was sent.

For a familiar chị/em thread, a suitable pattern is: "Chào chị, thứ Ba em qua gặp bà khoảng 12 giờ trưa hoặc 3 giờ chiều được không?" Use this only when the relationship and requested times fit; do not default to cô/con.

## Scope

Use outgoing messages as style evidence, not the other person's writing. Match the recipient's established language unless Jason specifies another. Keep only style preferences in this skill, not patient identifiers, phone numbers, or clinical details. A request to draft or remember a style is not authorization to send; use the authorization supplied for the current messaging task. Incorporate later explicit corrections to this style narrowly.
